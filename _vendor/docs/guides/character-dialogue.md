# Character voice and Japanese dialogue

Use explicit gender, age, personality, speech, fixed details and the relationship as character evidence. Gender and age are optional; if absent, use explicit persona details without inferring them from names, IDs or images. Do not invent demographic facts. Explicit speaking-style instructions and examples take priority over demographic conventions.

Before drafting dialogue, establish each speaker's language, first-person choice (if needed), way of addressing the listener, ordinary politeness level, vocabulary, sentence length, emotional restraint and characteristic rhythm. Keep this voice consistent across shots. Adapt courtesy to the actual listener and situation; a student addressing a driver need not speak the same way as when addressing a close friend. A change in register should have a contextual reason.

Japanese 2D visuals do not imply Japanese dialogue. Follow the dialogue language requested in the brief or revision; use Korean when none is specified. Direction and explanations remain Korean during storyboard generation. Only dialogue uses its requested language. Video conversion retains its English-instructions/original-dialogue policy.

For Japanese, write idiomatic spoken Japanese directly rather than translating Korean sentence patterns literally. Check particles, conjugation, tense/aspect, collocations, omitted subjects, reference clarity, question/response coherence and sentence-final expressions in context. Do not repeat personal pronouns or the listener's name when omission is natural. Respect explicit pronoun choices; gender alone does not determine a mandatory pronoun or ending. Avoid forced feminine/masculine suffixes, elderly speech, archaic forms, dialect or anime catchphrases unless the persona requests them. Fictional role language is a deliberate style, not evidence that every person of a gender or age speaks that way.

Preserve the intended facts, speaker, intention, emotional progression and ending while improving language. Do not add plot information, reverse a negation, change who did what, or invent a reply. During generation, allow plausible speech time; during language polishing, do not change timing, camera, delivery slots or line allocation. Never shorten a line just to fit its shot. User-supplied exact wording and locked shots remain authoritative; report concerns instead of changing them without authorization.

During semantic review, flag specific grammar, unnatural phrasing, unjustified register changes, inconsistent self-reference or age/persona mismatch by shot and line, with a suggested alternative that preserves meaning. Do not call an unusual but intentional character voice ungrammatical merely because it differs from a stereotype.
